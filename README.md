# HR Analytics - Excel Dashboard

## Project Overview

The HR Analytics project is an Excel-based data analysis and dashboard project focused on understanding employee demographics, attrition patterns, job characteristics, compensation, satisfaction, and employee experience.

The project uses Microsoft Excel to analyze employee-level HR data and transform it into meaningful calculations, summaries, and dashboard visualizations.

The analysis focuses on employee attrition, age groups, departments, job roles, monthly income, overtime, job satisfaction, work-life balance, years at company, and other workforce-related factors.

## Objective

The main objectives of this project are to:

- Analyze employee demographic characteristics.
- Understand employee attrition patterns.
- Analyze attrition across different departments.
- Examine attrition across employee age groups.
- Analyze attrition by job role.
- Understand employee income patterns.
- Analyze employee job satisfaction and involvement.
- Examine overtime and its relationship with attrition.
- Analyze employee tenure and years at company.
- Understand workforce distribution across departments.
- Identify important HR patterns and trends.
- Present HR analysis through an interactive Excel dashboard.
- Generate meaningful insights to support HR decision-making.

## Analysis & Key Findings

### Employee Attrition Analysis

The dataset contains **1,470 employees**.

The analysis shows:

- Employees who did not leave: **1,233**
- Employees who left: **237**
- Overall attrition rate: **16.12%**

This provides an overall view of employee retention and attrition within the dataset.

### Age Analysis

Employees were grouped into the following age categories:

- 18–24
- 25–31
- 32–38
- 39–45
- 46–52
- 53–60

The **32–38** age group contains the largest number of employees, with **451 employees**.

The **25–31** age group contains **358 employees**, while the **39–45** group contains **291 employees**.

### Attrition by Age Group

Among employees who left the organization, the largest number of attrition cases came from:

- 25–31: **80 employees**
- 32–38: **56 employees**
- 18–24: **38 employees**
- 39–45: **29 employees**
- 46–52: **21 employees**
- 53–60: **13 employees**

This shows that attrition cases are distributed across different age groups, with the 25–31 group contributing the largest number of exits in this dataset.

### Department Analysis

The dataset contains three major departments:

- Human Resources
- Research & Development
- Sales

Research & Development represents the largest department in the dataset.

The department-level analysis also compares employees who stayed with employees who left.

### Job Role Analysis

Attrition was analyzed across different job roles.

The roles with notable numbers of attrition cases include:

- Laboratory Technician
- Sales Executive
- Research Scientist
- Sales Representative
- Human Resources
- Manufacturing Director
- Healthcare Representative
- Manager
- Research Director

Laboratory Technician accounts for the largest share of the recorded attrition cases in the calculations.

### Average Age

The average employee age is approximately **36.92 years**.

This provides an overall view of the workforce age profile.

### Monthly Income Analysis

The average monthly income is approximately **6,502.93** in the dataset's recorded income units.

Monthly income can be analyzed together with job level, job role, and attrition to understand compensation-related workforce patterns.

### Years at Company

The average years employees have spent at the company is approximately **7.01 years**.

Average years at company by department are approximately:

| Department | Average Years at Company |
|---|---:|
| Human Resources | 7.24 |
| Research & Development | 6.86 |
| Sales | 7.28 |
| Overall | 7.01 |

### Overtime Analysis

The dataset contains information about whether employees work overtime.

Overtime can be compared with attrition to investigate whether employees working overtime show different attrition patterns.

### Employee Satisfaction

The project contains employee satisfaction measures including:

- Job Satisfaction
- Environment Satisfaction
- Relationship Satisfaction
- Work-Life Balance

These factors can be analyzed together with attrition to understand employee experience.

## Analysis & Key Insights

Based on the calculations and analysis in the Excel workbook, the following key insights were identified:

- The dataset contains **1,470 employees**.
- **237 employees** have an attrition status of Yes.
- **1,233 employees** have an attrition status of No.
- The overall attrition rate is approximately **16.12%**.
- The average employee age is approximately **36.92 years**.
- The average monthly income is approximately **6,502.93**.
- The **32–38** age group is the largest employee group with **451 employees**.
- The **25–31** age group has the highest number of recorded attrition cases, with **80 employees**.
- Research & Development is the largest department in the dataset.
- Laboratory Technician has the largest share of the recorded attrition cases among the listed job roles.
- The average employee tenure at the company is approximately **7.01 years**.
- Sales has the highest average years at company among the three departments at approximately **7.28 years**, followed by Human Resources at **7.24 years** and Research & Development at **6.86 years**.
- Overtime, job satisfaction, work-life balance, job role, department, and employee tenure provide useful dimensions for further analysis of employee attrition.

These findings are based on the calculations and data available in the Excel workbook.

## Recommendations

Based on the analysis performed, the following recommendations can be considered:

- Monitor attrition patterns across different age groups.
- Examine job roles with higher numbers of employee exits.
- Monitor department-level attrition and workforce distribution.
- Analyze overtime patterns together with employee attrition.
- Regularly monitor job satisfaction and work-life balance.
- Review compensation patterns across different job roles and job levels.
- Monitor employee tenure and retention patterns.
- Investigate factors affecting younger employee groups where attrition cases are higher.
- Use HR analytics regularly to identify changing workforce patterns.
- Combine multiple HR metrics rather than relying on attrition rate alone when investigating employee retention.
